**Goal:** Stratlas measures a site the way a surveyor and a site engineer do, as well as Propeller Aero does, offline. Every polygon can hold one or more comparisons from any surface to any surface: a survey, a design, a flat reference level, a triangulated base or a base the person edits vertex by vertex, with cut, fill, net and total, a deadband, and 3D, 2D and contour heat maps. One engine serves construction earthworks, mines and quarries, and landfill, each with its own template set. Typed measurement tools, units per measurement (US survey feet included), multi-surface cross-sections, design surfaces with tolerance checks and stationing, terrain overlays, terrain cleanup, QA of any delivered surface, and earthworks, stockpile and landfill reports sit on top. Coordinates match the surveyor's controller: any EPSG grid with its vertical datum and regional geoid, or a local site calibration imported from Trimble or 12d, applied the same way to every survey, design, measurement and export. Designs and results travel to and from Autodesk Civil 3D, Trimble Business Center and machine control, and 12d Model through LandXML, DXF, 12da and site-CRS GeoTIFF, LAZ and CSV. After the core: hydrology, haul-road compliance and local AI helpers.

## What the founder asked, and what it means

2. **Markets.** Construction earthworks (cut and fill to design, progress), mines and quarries (stockpiles, end-of-month volumes, benches) and landfill (airspace, compaction and density) are served to equal depth: one shared engine, one template set per industry.
3. **Core scope.**

Cut, fill, net and total; deadband; 3D, 2D and contour heat maps; several comparisons per polygon. Built by unifying the stockpile kit (`packages/volumetric`, `volumetric.build`) and M8 `change.surface`, not as a third engine.
   - Typed measurement tools, templates and custom fields; units per measurement including US survey feet; shrink and swell, weight and density calculators.
   - Multi-surface cross-sections: pins, grade readouts in degrees, ratio and percent, vertical exaggeration, DXF export.
   - Drawing aids: snapping, angle lock, typed distances, clamp to terrain.
   - Design surfaces and compliance: tolerance heat maps, vertical offset, alignments with stationing.
   - Survey timeline and comparison; contours, slope and elevation ramp overlays; terrain cleanup and crop; QA levels and checkpoint checks of any delivered DSM.
   - Earthworks, stockpile and landfill reports (PDF and CSV) through the existing report system.
4. **Later phases inside M11, after the core:** (1) hydrology: flood to level, runoff paths, catchments, direct rainfall; (2) haul-road compliance: width, gradient, cross fall, superelevation, berm height against limits; (3) local AI helpers: magic polygon boundary suggestions and an AI cut and fill area breakdown, on the M8 ONNX runtime, with commercially licensed weights only. The order is decision 6.
5. **Out:** DirtMate machine tracking; AeroPoints, GNSS hardware and the corrections network; cloud sharing, Crew links, push-to integrations and a public API (reframed as customer packages and file exports); Propeller CAD's AI design generation.
6. **Interop targets:** Autodesk Civil 3D, Trimble Business Center with Trimble machine control, 12d Model. Formats: LandXML (surfaces and alignments, in and out), DXF (in and out, through ezdxf, MIT, verified below), TTM (Trimble), 12da (12d ASCII), and GeoTIFF, LAZ and CSV in the site CRS. Trimble machine-control `.vcl` and `.dsz` depend on how open they are (decision 2). DWG: LibreDWG is GPL, so DXF only; native DWG through the paid ODA SDK is decision 1. IFC is deferred (IfcOpenShell LGPL-3.0, web-ifc MPL-2.0).
7. **Coordinates:** both full EPSG national and state grids with vertical datums and regional geoid models (beyond M10's global EGM96 and EGM2008), and local site calibrations imported from Trimble `.cal` and `.jxl` and 12d transforms, applied to every survey, design, measurement and export, so the numbers match the surveyor's controller.

Five findings shape the plan:

1. **Two good engines, neither general.** The stockpile kit computes four bases (`tin`, `plane`, `avg`, `low` in `packages/schema/src/volumes.ts`) on the kit's 10 cm grids in a web worker (`packages/volumetric/src/model/volume.ts`, `edit.ts`, `compute.ts`), but only inside a volumetric project. `change.surface` (`python/src/aio_pipelines/change/surface.py`) computes cut and fill between any two DSMs or clouds with a deadband, but has no design surface, and its `areas` input (`ChangeSurfaceParams.areas` in `packages/schema/src/jobs.ts`) is never sent by the UI. M11 writes one formula specification, one Python reference core and one TypeScript interactive executor, and makes both existing engines clients of them (see "The surface engine").
2. **Geodesy needs one truth.** Today `packages/geo/src/index.ts` `crsDefinition` knows WGS84 UTM, 4326 and 3857 only, `Measurement.unit` (`packages/schema/src/annotation.ts`) is `m`, `m2` or `deg`, and a project's vertical datum is one offset (`VerticalDatum.absAltOffsetM`; KNOWN-LIMITS "No geoid model"). Matching a controller to the millimetre needs PROJ grids and calibrations. M11 makes PROJ in the pack the only source of truth and has the renderer interpolate tables PROJ wrote, instead of re-implementing datums in proj4js (see "Coordinates, geoids and site calibration").
3. **The closed formats are the interop risk, not the open ones.** LandXML, DXF and 12da are documented text formats. TTM, `.vcl`, `.dsz` and Trimble `.cal` have no public specification we could find on 7 Oct 2026; LandXML is the path every target product reads and writes. Decision 2 decides how far we go.
4. **The hydrology libraries are copyleft.** pysheds is GPL-3.0+ and RichDEM GPL-3.0 (verified on PyPI and GitHub); WhiteboxTools is MIT. Our own flow code on numpy and SciPy is small (decision 3).
5. **Additive contracts again.** A new layer kind, raster `role`, `ProjectType` or `LayerDerived.kind` makes a 0.9 build refuse the whole manifest (`docs/release/UPGRADE-POLICY.md` rule 2; raster `role` is `z.enum(['ortho', 'dsm', 'plan'])` in `packages/schema/src/layers.ts`, `ProjectType` is a strict enum in `builder.ts`). So M11 keeps everything in a new `<project>/survey/` folder of side files that older builds never read, adds no project type (survey tools work in every project type), and puts overlays and prepared surfaces beside the manifest, not in it.

IFC (read-only)") as deferred; and adds LandXML, 12da and TTM to section 8 (decision 10).

## Global constraints (principles)

- **Offline first, zero network.**
  - Every computation runs locally: the pipeline pack for heavy work, a renderer worker for interactive volumes and sections, main's ONNX runtime for AI helpers. Nothing calls an elevation, geoid, CRS or "magic" service.
  - EPSG definitions and geoid grids ship in the pack and in geoid packs (`packs/geoid/`); a missing grid is an exact refusal ("needs the AUSGeoid2020 geoid pack"), never a silent fallback to the ellipsoid and never a download from PROJ's CDN (`PROJ_NETWORK=OFF` in the pack's environment, asserted in a test).
  - Downloading a geoid pack or an AI model pack is an explicit online action, as `packs:download` is, refused when the workstation is offline-only.
  - The zero-network e2e guard stays green with every survey tool open.
- **Licences: permissive only (M10 decision 1).** Apache-2.0, MIT, BSD, ISC, Zlib, BSL-1.0, PSF and similar; public-domain, CC-BY-4.0 and open-government data with attribution. Never GPL, AGPL, SSPL, non-commercial or research-only code or weights; LGPL only as the shared libraries already approved; MPL-2.0 only by name with approval. The M10 gate (`tools/release/license-check.mjs`, `python/tests/test_licences.py`, `native-licences.mjs`, `data-licences.mjs`, `licence-exceptions.json`) covers every M11 addition; geoid grids and AI weights go through the data and model gates.
- **A person decides.**
  - AI boundary suggestions and automatic cut and fill regions are drafts until a person accepts them (as `change.surface` regions are proposals today).
  - A calibration import shows its residuals and is applied only when a person confirms; changing the site CRS, datum or calibration never silently changes a reported number: every result records what it was computed with and shows **Stale, recompute** when that changes.
  - A QA level that fails puts the survey on hold with a banner; a person releases it. Nothing is deleted.
- **Never modify delivered data in place.**
  - Terrain cleanups and crops write a new derived surface in `survey/surfaces/`; the delivered DSM, cloud and ortho stay as they are.
  - Imported design files are copied into `survey/designs/<id>/` byte for byte and never rewritten; a vertical offset or archived layer is a setting in `designs.json`.
  - Packages are read-only: player mode shows measurements, sections and reports, and never starts a pipeline or writes a measurement.
- **SI inside, units at the edges.** Every stored value is metres, square metres, cubic metres, kilograms and tonnes per cubic metre, float64 in JSON and Python. Units are a display and export choice (site default, per measurement override). The international foot (0.3048 m) and the US survey foot (1200/3937 m) are distinct units with distinct labels, never "ft" for both.
- **Contracts are additive.**
  - No new layer kind, raster `role`, `ProjectType`, `LayerDerived.kind` or `Settings` field. Survey state lives in `<project>/survey/` (new families, `versions.ts` rows, `since: '0.11'`), user defaults in a new userData file `survey-defaults.json`.
  - New journal op kinds (`measurement.*`, `design.*`, `survey.settings`) are read by 0.9 and 0.10 as `unknown-kind` (`packages/journal/src/query.ts`), never refused; G0 pins this in a test.
  - Every change gets a row in `docs/architecture/contract-changes.md` before it merges.
- **No client data, ever.** Tests, fixtures, demos and screenshots use the synthetic surfaces, designs and calibrations of G13. Real survey data, real designs and real controller files are used only on the founder's machine through `@realdata` tests on a temporary copy (`apps/desktop/e2e/realData.ts`). `check-no-client-data` is extended to LandXML, DXF, 12da, JobXML and CSV (no real coordinates outside the fictional site, no real names, no real job numbers).
- **Heavy work off the UI thread.** Preparing surfaces, whole-site comparisons, contours, exports, hydrology and haul-road runs are pipeline jobs, resumable and cancellable. Interactive polygon volumes and sections run in a web worker on prepared tiles. AI inference runs in main's inference utility process.
- **Tests.** Windows stay off-screen (`STRATLAS_USER_DATA`); e2e with `--workers=1` locally. Every volume, area, grade and transform is asserted against an analytic or PROJ truth (see "Quality targets"). `pnpm check`, `pnpm test:e2e`, `uv run pytest` and `pack-native` must be green before each merge.

## Review focus

- **Volume semantics.** Sign convention (`dz = To - From`; fill where `dz > 0`, cut where `dz < 0`, net = fill - cut, total = fill + cut), partial cells at polygon edges (coverage-weighted, never centre-in-or-out), the deadband (cells with `|dz| < deadband` contribute nothing when "use in calculations" is on, and the report says so), nodata (a comparison over holes reports the uncovered area and refuses above a threshold rather than treating holes as zero), and each base's exact definition. The stockpile kit's numbers stay identical.
- **Geodesy.** Axis order, grid versus ground distances (a scale factor of 0.9996 is 40 cm per kilometre), US survey versus international feet, ellipsoidal versus orthometric heights, the geoid named on every result and export, calibrations applied in the controller's order (projection, horizontal similarity, vertical inclined plane), and one code path (PROJ) for every number.
- **Two executors, one formula.** The renderer worker and the Python core agree to 1e-6 relative on shared fixtures; any difference is a bug in one of them, not a tolerance to widen.
- **Stale results.** A result is never shown as current after a surface, design offset, base, deadband, calibration or geoid changes; the fingerprint is part of the result.
- **Interop fidelity.** A LandXML surface or alignment exported and imported back is identical; a DXF 3DFACE surface imported into Civil 3D and TBC lands at the same coordinates (founder matrix); stationing matches the alignment's own start station and equations.
- **Hostile inputs.** LandXML with entity expansion, external entities or a 10 GB declared size; DXF with a million entities or binary DXF; 12da with broken records; CSV with mixed separators, a BOM and latitude-longitude swapped; JobXML with unknown elements: exact errors, never a crash of main, never an unbounded allocation.
- **Performance on ordinary hardware.** Interactive polygon volumes on 4 M cells under 300 ms on the reference laptop; a 100 ha site at 5 cm (400 M cells) through the pipeline in bounded memory with progress.

## The surface engine: options and recommendation

### What exists

|          | Stockpile kit (`packages/volumetric`, `volumetric.build`)                                | M8 `change.surface`                                                 |
| -------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Inputs   | Kit grids (`vs-kit-js`, 10 cm piles, 0.4 m site DSM) of a volumetric project             | Any two DSM (`cog` or `aio.grid/1`) or cloud layers                 |
| Bases    | `tin`, `plane`, `avg`, `low` from the toe line                                           | None: survey to survey only                                         |
| Polygons | Toe lines, edited in the boundary editor; volumes recomputed in a worker (`editVolumes`) | `areas` in the contract, never sent by the UI                       |
| Outputs  | `volumes.json`, register CSV, house-report volumes section                               | Change set (`aio.change/1`), heat map pyramid, regions with volumes |
| Strength | Interactive, exact on its grids                                                          | Any surface, registration check, site totals                        |

### Options

1. **Extend the TypeScript kit engine to everything.** Interactive, but it would need COG, COPC and TIN readers in the renderer and would duplicate `change.surface`'s gridding and registration.
2. **Extend `change.surface` to everything.** One code path, but every polygon edit would be a pipeline round trip (seconds), which kills drawing and vertex editing.
3. **One formula specification, two executors on one prepared surface format (recommended).** A Python reference core (`python/src/aio_pipelines/survey/compare.py`) and a TypeScript interactive executor (`packages/survey/src/engine/`), both implementing the same written specification (data-conventions section 25), both reading surfaces prepared once by `survey.prepare` into height tiles. `change.surface` keeps its contract and calls the core for its volumes; the stockpile kit's four bases become four of the general bases and the volumetric workspace calls the TypeScript executor. Shared fixtures in `packages/schema/src/__fixtures__/survey/` are run by both and must agree to 1e-6 relative.

**Recommendation: option 3** (ADR 0009, written by G0). It is "unify, not a third engine" in practice: one definition, one surface format, one set of fixtures; the two existing engines become clients rather than being replaced by a parallel one.

### Surfaces and bases

A comparison item is `{ from: SurfaceRef, to: SurfaceRef, deadbandM?, useDeadband, cellM? }`. `SurfaceRef` is one of:

| Kind                  | Meaning                                                                                                                                                                                        | Source                                                                                                                             |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `survey`              | A capture's DSM (or DTM, or a cleaned derived surface)                                                                                                                                         | `raster` layer with `role: 'dsm'`, a cloud gridded by `survey.prepare`, or `survey/surfaces/<id>`                                  |
| `current`, `previous` | The survey the measurement is viewed on, and the one before it                                                                                                                                 | Resolved through `captureIndex()` (`packages/workspace/src/captures.ts`) at compute time; the result records which capture it used |
| `design`              | A design surface layer, with its vertical offset                                                                                                                                               | `survey/designs/<id>/` TIN                                                                                                         |
| `reference`           | A flat level: a typed elevation, or **highest** or **lowest** of the From surface along the polygon perimeter (`perimeter-max`, `perimeter-min`) or inside it (`interior-max`, `interior-min`) | Computed                                                                                                                           |
| `smart`               | Delaunay TIN of the perimeter densified at the cell size and sampled on the From surface (the kit's `tin`)                                                                                     | Computed                                                                                                                           |
| `fit-plane`           | Least-squares plane through the perimeter samples (the kit's `plane`)                                                                                                                          | Computed                                                                                                                           |
| `perimeter-mean`      | Flat level at the mean perimeter height (the kit's `avg`)                                                                                                                                      | Computed                                                                                                                           |
| `custom`              | A TIN of the polygon's vertices with heights the person edits (N, E, elevation or offset per vertex; multi-select; move all)                                                                   | Stored on the measurement                                                                                                          |

The kit's `low` maps to `reference` `perimeter-min`. A volumetric project's four bases show under their old names; `volumes.json` is unchanged.

**Volume formula (grid):** on a grid of cell `c` aligned to the prepared tiles, each cell `i` gets a coverage weight `w_i` (exact polygon-cell intersection area over `c²`, computed by clipping, not a centre test), `dz_i = To(i) - From(i)` sampled bilinearly at the cell centre (TIN surfaces barycentrically). Fill = `c² Σ w_i max(dz_i, 0)`, cut = `c² Σ w_i max(-dz_i, 0)` over cells with `|dz_i| >= deadband` when the deadband is used. Cells where either surface has no data are counted as `uncoveredM2`; above 2% of the polygon the item reports "partly outside the survey" and above 20% it refuses. **TIN to TIN** (design to design, design to reference or custom base) is computed exactly by intersecting the two triangulations (prismoidal), no grid.

**Outputs per item:** cut, fill, net, total (m³), area cut, area fill, area unchanged, uncovered area, From and To descriptions, the capture ids used, the deadband, the cell, and a fingerprint of every input (surface file hashes, design offset, base parameters, calibration and geoid ids). Calculators (shrink and swell, density, weight) are applied at display time from the material and never change the stored volume.

## Coordinates, geoids and site calibration

**One truth.** Every number a person reads or exports comes from PROJ in the pack (pyproj), with the site's horizontal CRS, vertical datum, geoid grid and calibration applied in one pipeline, `python/src/aio_pipelines/geodesy/site.py`. The renderer never re-implements a datum:

- **Horizontal readouts** use proj4js only when the CRS is a pure projection proj4js represents exactly (checked by comparing it with PROJ at 25 points over the site at G1's build time); otherwise, and for every calibrated site, `survey.prepare` writes `survey/geodesy/site-transform.json` plus a binary grid that maps the project frame to the site grid at 1 m spacing (float64), interpolated bilinearly in the renderer.
- **Vertical readouts** use a geoid subgrid for the site extent (`survey/geodesy/geoid-site.f64`), cut from the geoid pack by PROJ.
- **Parity test:** renderer readouts equal pyproj within 1 mm horizontally and vertically at 1,000 random points of each synthetic site; it runs in CI for every fixture CRS and calibration.

**Site settings** (`survey/settings.json`, `aio.survey-settings/1`): the horizontal CRS (any EPSG code or WKT), the vertical datum (an EPSG vertical CRS with a named geoid pack, `ellipsoidal`, or `calibration`), an optional calibration, display units (distance, area, volume, density, mass, grade style), coordinate order (NEZ or ENZ), precision (decimal places per quantity), the industry template sets in use, the site materials list, the QA level, and default heat map stops and deadband. The manifest `crs` stays what the data is stored in; the site settings say how it is shown and exported. Changing either marks dependent results stale.

**EPSG catalogue.** A build step (`tools/geo/build-crs-catalogue.mjs` calling `python -m aio_pipelines --crs-catalogue`) reads `proj.db` and writes `packages/geo/src/catalogue/epsg.json.gz` (projected, geographic and vertical CRSs: code, name, area of use, units, datum, deprecated flag, and a PROJ string when proj4js can represent it). `searchCrs` (`packages/geo/src/crs.ts`) searches it by code, name and area, and suggests the CRSs whose area of use contains the project origin. Size budget: under 1.5 MB compressed in the renderer chunk (lazy).

**Geoid packs** (`<data>/packs/geoid/<id>.tif` and `<id>.json`, `aio.geoid-pack/1`: name, region bbox, horizontal and vertical datum EPSG codes, PROJ file name, licence, attribution, provenance, sha256). The pack's PROJ search path includes the folder. **Settings, Map packs** lists them with coverage; import from a USB folder or a configured download list, as raster packs do.

**Local site calibration** (`survey/calibration.json`, `aio.site-calibration/1`):

- **Model:** the controller's order: geographic to a base projection (often transverse Mercator at the site with a scale factor), a horizontal similarity (translation, rotation, scale; Helmert 2D), and a vertical adjustment (constant shift plus an inclined plane with slopes north and east about an origin), with or without a geoid. Parameters, the point pairs and their residuals (horizontal and vertical) are stored; the source file is kept by hash.
- **Import (`geo.calibration` pipeline):** Trimble JobXML (`.jxl`: the calibration and the points), Trimble `.dc` (text), 12d transforms (from a 12da or the parameters 12d reports), Trimble `.cal` if decision 8 allows; or **Compute from point pairs** (the person enters or imports pairs of WGS84 or grid coordinates and local coordinates, and Stratlas solves the same model by least squares).
- **Confirm:** the residual table is shown with the controller's own residuals side by side where the file has them; applying it is a person's action, journaled, and marks dependent results stale.
- **Applied everywhere:** readouts, measurement coordinates, design import (a design in local coordinates is placed through the calibration), exports ("site grid" means calibrated), reports (each states "Site calibration X, residuals H n mm, V n mm").

- **JobXML calibration elements** (checked in schema 6.27 and 6.30):
  - The root is `JOBFile`.
  - `Environment/CoordinateSystem` holds `SystemName`, `ZoneName`, `DatumName`, the EPSG fields, `Ellipsoid`, `Projection`, `LocalSite`, `Datum`, `HorizontalAdjustment`, `VerticalAdjustment`, `CombinedScaleFactor` and `CalibrationPointPairs`.
  - `HorizontalAdjustment` holds `Type` (`NoAdjustment` or `PlaneAdjustment`), `OriginNorth`, `OriginEast`, `TranslationNorth`, `TranslationEast`, `Rotation` (decimal degrees) and `ScaleFactor`.
  - `VerticalAdjustment` holds `Type` (`NoAdjustment`, `InclinedPlane`, `GeoidModel` or `GeoidModelAndInclinedPlane`), `OriginNorth`, `OriginEast`, `SlopeNorthPerUnit`, `SlopeEastPerUnit`, `ConstantAdjustment` and `GeoidName`.
  - `FieldBook` repeats the adjustments as `HorizontalAdjustmentRecord` and `VerticalAdjustmentRecord`.
  - `FieldBook` also holds `CalibrationPointRecord` (`GridPointName`, `WGS84PointName`, `Dimension`, `HorizontalResidual`, `VerticalResidual`) and the `PointRecord`s of both points of each pair.
  - The separate `HelmertTransformationDefinition` elements are COGO transformations, not the site calibration.
- **Developer terms.** The [Trimble Developer Terms](https://trimble.com/legal/developer-terms) (v2.1, updated 26 Jun 2026) cover API and SDK toolkits only. They forbid reverse engineering and "substantially replicating" Trimble offerings, and grant no rights to TTM, VCL or `.cal`. None of the closed formats above is to be implemented without a written agreement.
- **Recommendation:**
  - Export full-TIN LandXML for surfaces (G7).
  - Import JobXML for calibrations, which Siteworks also takes as a calibration source.
  - Read and write 12da from the 12d manual (G6, G7).
  - Ask Trimble for a written agreement on TTM, VCL and DC only if a customer needs them.
- **Open:**
  - Does the founder accept dropping `.dc` from decision 8, as recorded here, or want a Trimble request first?
  - The JobXML sign conventions (rotation, and which coordinates the inclined plane uses) need a check against a real controller job (founder test step, decision 9). The importer tries the readings that are possible, keeps the one that reproduces the file's own residuals, and records which one it chose.
